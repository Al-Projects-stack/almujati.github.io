The difference is in what we leave out, and how long we're willing to wait.

We pour a coconut-soy blend, never paraffin, which burns cooler and longer, and skips the petroleum soot that greys the rim of a cheaper jar. The wick is braided cotton, trimmed to throw scent evenly from the first light to the last.

Every candle carries up to a ten percent fragrance load, blended by a trained nose rather than dropped in from a catalogue. Then the hard part: we cure each jar for two full weeks before it ships, so the scent has set into the wax and fills a room instead of just the air above the flame.
